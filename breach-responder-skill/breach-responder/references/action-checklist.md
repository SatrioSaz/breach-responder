# Turning exposure into an action checklist

Add the matching actions only for categories that were confirmed in the exposure list (high or medium confidence). Every item is a suggestion for the user to act on: this skill never changes an account, password or external service.

## Risk order

Number the checklist in this order, highest risk first:

1. Login credentials
2. Payment-card details, bank account
3. Passport or national ID number, government or tax identifier, signature or photographed identity document
4. Health or insurance identifier
5. Date of birth, home or mailing address, full name
6. Email address, phone number

Each line states the action, the category behind it, and the source file, for example:
`1. Change your Acme Insurance password and any reused ones; turn on two-step verification. (Login credentials · notes/acme-account.txt)`

## Actions by category

- **Login credentials**: change the password for this company's account and anywhere the same password was reused; turn on two-step verification; review recent account activity.
- **Payment-card details**: check statements for charges you do not recognise; set up transaction alerts; ask the bank whether the card should be replaced.
- **Bank account**: watch for unexpected debits and new payees; contact the bank if anything looks wrong.
- **Passport or national ID number**: note the exposure for any identity-fraud follow-up; ask the issuing authority whether it offers a watch or replacement process.
- **Government or tax identifier**: watch for accounts or filings opened in your name; consider a fraud alert or credit freeze where available.
- **Signature or photographed identity document**: high value for impersonation; monitor closely for accounts opened in your name.
- **Health or insurance identifier**: check insurance statements for claims you did not make.
- **Date of birth, home or mailing address, full name**: together these raise identity-fraud risk; watch for mail about accounts or loans you did not open.
- **Email address**: expect more phishing; be careful with messages that mention this company and go to its official site directly.
- **Phone number**: expect scam calls and texts; do not act on unsolicited contact claiming to be the company; ask your carrier about a SIM-swap or port-out lock.

## Always add at the end

- Read the company's official breach notice to see which data was affected.
- Decide whether to keep the account; if not, ask the company to close it and delete your data.

Keep needs-human-review items in their own list, never in the checklist, so they are not mistaken for confirmed exposure.
